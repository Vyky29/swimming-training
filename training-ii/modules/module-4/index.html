MODULE 4 — Core Aquatic Skills

Following the early aquatic experiences introduced in Module 3, swimmers begin to develop the core aquatic skills that underpin safe, controlled and effective movement in the water.

This module explores how breathing control, floating and balance, gliding and streamlining, and longitudinal rotation develop through structured support, progressive learning and repeated successful experiences.

MODULE 4 — Core Aquatic Skills

Module 4 builds upon the foundations, teaching approaches and early aquatic experiences developed throughout the previous modules.

The focus now shifts towards developing the core aquatic skills that allow swimmers to regulate, control their body, move through the water and organise their body effectively within the aquatic environment.

These skills form the bridge between early aquatic participation and future swimming development.

Block 1 — Breathing Control & Submersion

Breathing control and submersion form the foundation of aquatic learning.

This block explores how swimmers develop confidence with water contact, breathing and immersion, and how these skills support future aquatic development.

Through structured support, progressive learning and repeated successful experiences, swimmers learn to interact with the aquatic environment with increasing confidence, control and independence.

Understand how breathing and submersion influence confidence, regulation and participation.
•

•

•
Recognise when swimmers are ready to progress, consolidate or reduce demand.

              Block 3 — Gliding & Streamlining

              Gliding and streamlining are fundamental aquatic skills that enable swimmers to move efficiently through the water using body position, alignment and momentum.

              This block explores how swimmers develop controlled movement, reduce resistance and maintain efficient body positions, and how these skills prepare them for propulsion and coordinated swimming. Gliding acts as the bridge between floating and active propulsion, transforming stable body control into controlled movement through the water. Through structured progression, repeated successful experiences and gradual reduction of support, swimmers learn to maintain alignment, control and efficient movement throughout the aquatic environment.

              Learning Outcomes

              By the end of this block, instructors should be able to:

              •
              Understand how gliding and streamlining influence movement efficiency, body position and aquatic skill development.
              •
              Recognise the key variables that influence gliding and streamlining development.
              •
              Apply structured teaching approaches to develop gliding through body position, alignment and progressive challenge.
              •
              Recognise when to progress, consolidate or reduce demand during gliding and streamlining development.

                    Block 4 — Longitudinal Rotation

                    This block focuses on how swimmers develop the ability to change body position in the water through longitudinal rotation.

                    Longitudinal rotation is the ability to rotate around the body’s long axis while maintaining balance, orientation and control. It is a fundamental aquatic skill that helps swimmers recover stability, regulate movement and prepare for increasingly complex aquatic skills.

                    Learning Outcomes

                    By the end of this block, you will be able to:

                    •
                    Explain the role of longitudinal rotation in aquatic development.
                    •
                    Recognise when swimmers are ready to begin learning longitudinal rotation.
                    •
                    Develop longitudinal rotation using progressive teaching variables.
                    •
                    Apply the Rotation Development Model to create individualised learning experiences.
                    •
                    Understand how longitudinal rotation supports wider aquatic movement and future aquatic skills.

                    Inside This Block

                    This block is organised into three progressive sections that explore how swimmers understand, develop and apply longitudinal rotation within aquatic learning.

                    Section 1 — Understanding Rotation

                    3

                    Section 3 — Integration & Functional Application

                    • Understand how longitudinal rotation becomes integrated into functional aquatic movement and continues to support future swimming development.

                          In Practice

                          Key Ideas to Remember

                          Longitudinal rotation develops progressively.

                          Swimmers first learn to rotate with control before integrating the skill into wider aquatic movement.

                          The teaching sequence remains consistent.

                          Back-to-front rotation is introduced first, followed by front-to-back rotation and later linked rotation.

                          Body organisation changes the challenge.

                          Open Body, Soldier and Rocket positions progressively increase the level of control required.

                          The clubSENsational Teaching Principle guides progression.

                          Teaching variables are selected and combined to create individualised learning experiences rather than following one fixed progression.

                          Rotation supports future aquatic development.

                          Although isolated rotation practice decreases over time, the skill remains essential for efficient movement, transitions and future swimming stroke development.
